## Context

Milestone 2 left a Cessna flying on the phone: tilt, throttle, HUD, chase camera, a flat test field. Milestone 3 replaces it with the airplane the game is for.

- **The F-4C's JSBSim file,** built from real data and checked against it, with the real jet's feel system and stability augmentation.
- **The owner's own model,** `models/f4c.glb`, with moving surfaces, gear, flaps and speedbrakes.
- **Afterburner plumes,** the throttle's afterburner band, all four buttons.
- **One feel round carried over from the Cessna:** the takeoff roll.

**Done when (from `CLAUDE.md`):** the F-4C passes its checkride against the flight manual and NASA data, and the owner flies it on the test field and approves the feel.

Not in this milestone: Hawaii, the map, water, the steerpoint, random runways. Nothing from `docs/ideas.md` or issue #33 (the fleet).

**Evidence labels:** **Verified** = checked against something we didn't make. **Supported** = our own run shows it (planning runs were flown in Node on the committed WebAssembly build; nothing was written to the repo). **Reasoned** = not tested yet; the test that settles it is named.

An independent review of the first draft read the same sources and the code, and found eleven things: lines that could not pass or could not be measured as written, and facts I had missed. All are corrected here; the three that change what a pilot sees are the pull (8.5 g is more than the wing can lift at 450 knots: the line is at 480), the angle of attack (the flight manual's units have no scale to degrees, so the stall lines use AFWAL's degrees), and the engines (JSBSim's stock thrust table cannot reach Mach 2).

## 1. How others solved this

### An aircraft file built from wind-tunnel data
| What others did | Label |
|---|---|
| **Richard Harrison's FlightGear F-14 and F-15** (JSBSim). Wind-axis build-up: one named function per coefficient, summed per axis; tables in angle of attack and sideslip straight from the reports (AFWAL-TR-80-3141 for the F-14, an AFIT thesis for the F-15); Mach as a multiplier; damping terms as rate × table; gear, flaps and speedbrake as position × table; ground effect from a NASA report. Checked against the flight manual, not flight test. [f-14b.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Zaretto/fg-aircraft/master/aircraft/f-14b/f-14b.xml), [zaretto.com/f-14](https://zaretto.com/f-14) | Verified (read) |
| **AFWAL-TR-80-3141 Part III** (1981): a complete F-4J aerodynamic model as tables, −10° to 110° angle of attack, made from the NASA reports below and McDonnell data, with its own validation: wing rock, and a departure in a wind-up turn. [PDF](https://zaretto.com/sites/zaretto.com/files/F-14-data/ADA101648.pdf) | Verified (read) |
| **Zaretto F-4X** (Matthew Anderson, 2017): a JSBSim F-4S made from that report. "90%": no Mach tables, flap and speedbrake lift never summed in. [F-4S-fdm.xml](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Zaretto/F-4X/master/F-4S-fdm.xml) | Verified (read) |
| **NASA CR-2144** (Heffley and Jewell, 1972): the F-4C itself. Weight, balance and inertias; trim, lift and drag against Mach; every stability derivative at ten flight conditions from 136 knots at sea level to Mach 2.15; the periods and damping that result, augmentation off and on; and the feel system and stability augmentation as block diagrams with their numbers. [NTRS](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19730003312) | Verified (read; tables viewed as page images) |
| **NASA TN D-6425, D-5361, D-6091, D-7721:** the F-4 model's forces to 110°, the directional divergence, and the roll and yaw damping at high angle of attack. All on NTRS. | Verified (read) |
| JSBSim's `F4N` is Aeromatic's first guess (constants, an empty flight-control system). UIUC's F-4 is one flight condition of constants from Roskam's textbook. FlightGear's own F-4 is YASim. | Verified (read) |
| Heatblur's DCS F-4E: "based on actual performance data"; no sources published. | Verified (read) |

**Choice.** Harrison's structure, on the F-4's own data:
- **Shape with angle of attack and sideslip** from AFWAL-TR-80-3141's tables (they are the NASA reports, already reduced to a model and validated).
- **Change with Mach, and all the F-4C's own numbers** (weight, inertias, flaps-down condition, controls) from CR-2144.
- **Checked** against CR-2144's ten conditions, the flight manual, and the two reports' own validation runs.
- Departures from the F-4X, each with its reason: it is the F-4C, not the slatted F-4S; Mach effects are in; the flight controls are CR-2144's F-4C ones.

What this is not: an independent proof at CR-2144's conditions. The file is built from those numbers, so matching them shows it was built right. The independent checks are the flight manual and the AFWAL validation runs.

### The engines
| What others did | Label |
|---|---|
| JSBSim's own `turbine_engine`, afterburner method 2: throttle 0 to 1 is idle to MIL, 1 to 2 is the afterburner. Harrison's F-14 and F-15 and JSBSim's F-16 all use it. | Verified (read) |
| JSBSim v1.3.3 gives full afterburner thrust the instant it is asked, whatever the engine speed, and has no "afterburner on" number to read. [FGTurbine.cpp](https://github.com/JSBSim-Team/jsbsim/blob/v1.3.3/src/models/propulsion/FGTurbine.cpp) | Verified (read) |
| The J79-GE-15: 10,900 lb at MIL, 17,000 lb at Max, each, sea level static. T.O. 1F-4C-1, section I | Verified (read) |
| Harrison tuned his thrust tables to the flight manual's performance charts. | Verified (read) |

**Choice.** JSBSim's turbine as it is, with the J79-GE-15's two ratings. The aircraft file's own throttle system lights the afterburner only once the engine is at MIL speed, as the real one does, and the plumes read what that system gives. **How thrust falls off with speed and height is the one place a generic guess could get in:** nobody publishes it for the J79-GE-15. JSBSim's own J79 table (Aeromatic's kind) gives about half the thrust needed at Mach 1.8 and 55,000 ft and nothing at 60,000, so it cannot stand (the review's reading of it; Reasoned). The tables are anchored to CR-2144 instead: along its zero-excess-thrust line the engines' thrust must equal its drag, and at its other conditions thrust must be at least the drag. Between those anchors the shape is JSBSim's. PR 11 checks it.

**No existing F-4 model is copied.** The `F4N` is read for the file format only. The F-4X is a precedent, not a source: every number in FSG's file cites the report's own page.

### Tilt on a jet with no force feedback
| What others did | Label |
|---|---|
| The real F-4 limits the pilot by stick force: a bellows that stiffens the stick with airspeed and a bobweight that pushes back with g. CR-2144 gives both, with the stick-to-stabilator gearing (figures IV-3 to IV-5), and the pull per g that results (table IV-12): about 12 lb per g below Mach 1 (12.5 at sea level and Mach 0.8, 12.2 at 35,000 ft and Mach 0.9), 18 to 21 above it. | Verified (read) |
| Heatblur's F-4E for players with ordinary joysticks: "Stick Deflection Limit: limits the maximum force in lbf the pilot is able to pull the stick with… prevents accidentally pulling 20 G." [manual](https://f4.manuals.heatblur.se/dcs/special_options.html) | Verified (read) |
| Wings: full tilt gave about the g limit above corner speed; below it the wing stalled first. Done there by fading the elevator inside Wings' own physics. | Verified (the owner's phone, in Wings) |
| JSBSim's F-16 as flown in milestone 1: stick straight to the flight computer. Full back stick gave 9.8 g at 600 knots. | Supported (milestone 1) |

**Choice.** On the F-4C, **back tilt is a pull in pounds, and the jet's own feel system turns it into stabilator.** Full back tilt is the pull that holds 8.5 g at 480 knots at sea level (the flight manual's limit at 37,500 lb, up to Mach 0.72: about 95 lb). Slower than that the wing gives out first: CR-2144's most lift is about 7.9 g at 450 knots. Forward tilt is its own, smaller push. This is Wings' rule, reached the way the real jet reaches it, with nothing added to the physics. The feel system is FCS, in the aircraft file. Roll stays a stick position (the F-4's roll feel is a plain spring).

**Choice.** CR-2144's diagrams, written in JSBSim's flight-control format, with the actuator rates AFWAL-TR-80-3141 gives. Always on: the game has no switch for them.

### Checking it flies like an F-4
| What others did | Label |
|---|---|
| CR-2144 lists, for each of its ten conditions, the trim angle of attack and stabilator, and the period and damping of each motion. | Verified (read) |
| The flight manual, section VI: buffet from about 40 knots above the stall, wing rock from about 10 knots above it, the stall at 26 to 30 units as a nose slice; ailerons at the stall roll it the wrong way; rudder is "the best and safest means of rolling the aircraft at high AOA"; stick forward with ailerons neutral recovers. Sections I, II and V: thrust, approach angle of attack (19.2 units), limits. | Verified (read) |
| AFWAL-TR-80-3141's validation: controls-fixed wing rock, and a departure in a wind-up turn. | Verified (read) |
| The performance manual, T.O. 1F-4C-1-1: no open copy found. But the flight manual's own text carries the F-4E's performance appendix: approach 138 knots at 32,000 lb plus 2 knots per 1,000 lb (152 at 38,924 lb); landing roll at 30,000 lb on a wet runway 5,400 ft with the drag chute and 7,700 without; nosewheel lift-off 167 knots at 55,000 lb. And for the F-4C: the full-flap stall at 38,000 lb is 122 to 126 knots; flaps take 8 s down and 6 s up; a spin loses up to 2,000 ft a turn. Its charts (the g limit by weight, the negative limit) are pictures and are not in the text. | Verified (read); the F-4E's figures are a different engine and weight |
| The flight manual gives angle of attack in units, with no scale to degrees. AFWAL-TR-80-3141 gives the true angles: wing rock at 19° to 22°, the nose slice at 22° to 25.5°. CR-2144 gives 11.7° on approach. | Verified (read) |
| Milestone 1's rule for this milestone: numbers a pilot would recognise, within what a pilot can read (about 5 knots, 5%, 10% on g and roll rate); feel numbers reported with no pass or fail; when a number can't be met, one report in pilot terms and the owner's decision, never a week of tuning. | The owner's (20 November 2026) |

**Choice.** The checkride flies each maneuver headless and prints the result beside the reference and its tolerance, in knots, feet, degrees, seconds and g. The references go into one file, `tests/checkride/f4c-reference.js`, each with its source and page, before the airplane is flown against them.

**Measuring a motion.** CR-2144 gives each motion as the response to a control (its transfer-function tables). Many of them cannot be timed as a clean swing: with augmentation on, some are not swings at all, and on approach the quick and slow pitch motions run together. So the checkride gives the airplane a pulse and lays its response beside the one CR-2144's own table gives for the same pulse: time to the first peak, the size of the peak, and the time to die to half. This is how a simulation is matched to handling-qualities data (the tables are Heffley's for this purpose; Stevens, Lewis and Johnson check their F-16 model the same way).

**Flaps, gear, speedbrakes and ground effect.** CR-2144's power-approach condition gives gear and flaps together for the F-4C. How that splits between gear and flaps, the speedbrakes' drag, and ground effect have no F-4 source that I found. Harrison's F-14 and F-15 treat them the same way: position × a table, tuned to the flight manual. Here they are Reasoned, sized to the flight manual's approach and stall speeds, and reported as feel numbers beside Wings'.

## 2. What the Cessna's takeoff roll showed (the owner's note)

Flown headless from the numbers, full throttle, flaps down (Supported):

- **The cause is the engine's torque.** At full power it rolls the airplane left with 427 lb-ft. On the ground the wheels carry that: parked, the right main already carries less than the left. As the wing takes the weight the right main goes light first, **at 53 to 55 knots, every time.**
- **A 10° back tilt does not fly it off until 67 knots** (74 on the phone). The gentler middle sends only 18% elevator there. So for 15 to 20 knots it runs on the left main and the nosewheel. A 15° back tilt flies it off at 52 knots, all wheels together.
- **The one stick does three jobs.** 30% right stick for one second at 60 knots, to pick up the wing, also swings the nose 2.6° right. That is the "not enough control".
- **Today's automatic feet do not keep it straight.** If the pilot only holds the wings level, it is 49 ft off the centreline by liftoff: the feet remove sideslip, not a slow turn.
- Bank on the ground stays under 3° in every run. The roll trim (set for 100 knots) and the feet coming in at 20 to 40 knots are not the cause.

**The copilot's feet, as proposed** (tried with first-guess numbers; Supported):

| 30% right stick for 1 s, pilot otherwise hands-off | Today | Copilot's feet |
|---|---|---|
| at 20 knots (taxi: it should turn) | nose swings 13.1° | 7.0° |
| at 40 knots | 3.8° | 1.0° |
| at 60 knots (it should not) | 2.6° | 0.5° |

| Takeoff, 10° back tilt | Today | Copilot's feet |
|---|---|---|
| Pilot only holds the wings level | 49 ft off the centreline at liftoff | 26 ft |
| Pilot holds the wings and steers a little | 5 ft, 1.8° of bank | 5 ft, 1.1° |

Firmer feet held the hands-only takeoff to 16 ft and then 7 ft, but fought the pilot's steering at a taxi (2.5° and then nothing at 20 knots). So the feet must follow the pilot's steering, not fight it. A third try, which worked out the turn the nosewheel asks for from its angle and the speed, kept the taxi steering (14° at 20 knots) but swung 5.6° at 60 knots: worse than today. **No version tried yet meets every line of PR 2 at once.** All three treated the feet by themselves. The owner's question gives the way to think about it (PR 2): one curve shared by nosewheel, rudder and aileron, with the copilot's straight-keeping growing exactly as the pilot's steering share shrinks. PR 2 starts with throwaway runs of that; if no version meets the lines, it stops and reports what the best gives.

### PR 2. Feel, round 2: the copilot's feet on the ground; the debug overlay in one place. Version 0.0.17 (phone check, on the Cessna)
**In it**
**How to think about it (the owner's question, 26 November 2026): two jobs, three controls.** Direction is the nosewheel and the rudder. The wings are the ailerons. A real pilot gives one job to his feet and the other to his hands; ours has one tilt, so the tilt's meaning slides from steering to banking along the roll, on one smooth curve shared by all three controls.

What a right tilt does on the ground, control by control:

| | At a taxi | Mid-roll | Near liftoff |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ailerons** (the pilot's hands) | follow the tilt; no airflow, so nothing happens | follow the tilt; starting to bite | follow the tilt; they hold the wings |
| **Nosewheel** (the copilot's feet) | turns the airplane as the tilt asks | fading out | out |
| **Rudder** (the copilot's feet) | follows too, but no airflow, so nothing happens | taking over the turning from the nosewheel | eases the nose the way of the tilt, gently, and holds it straight otherwise |

- **The ailerons always get all of the tilt,** with no blending. Their bite grows with speed by itself.
- **The turn the tilt asks for** gets gentler with speed on a smooth curve with no step, and never switches off while a wheel is on the ground.
- **The copilot's feet make that turn with the nosewheel at low speed and with the rudder at higher speed,** handing over smoothly on one curve (the Cessna starts from Wings': the nosewheel at half by 29 knots).
- **The copilot's feet also hold it straight** against anything the tilt did not ask for (the engine's pull): against the yaw rate and the heading it has drifted from. In the air: Wings' feet, unchanged.
- An aircraft's settings choose this; `c172x` and `f16` keep what milestone 1 tested.
- **The debug overlay** sits in one place on every screen.

**Done when**
- `npm test`, on `c172p`:
  - **A 10° right tilt held for 3 s** on the roll, full throttle, flaps down (it sends 12% of the stick):
    - at 10 knots the nose comes right 5° or more, and the nosewheel is doing it: the nosewheel is at 90% or more of what that tilt gets standing still, and the rudder's deflection changes the turn by less than a tenth;
    - at 60 knots the nose eases right between 1° and 4°, and the rudder is doing it: the rudder is deflected right, and the nosewheel is at 20% or less of its standing-still angle;
    - at every speed the right aileron's trailing edge is up by the same amount, and the bank stays under 3°.
  - **The handover is smooth:** from 0 to 120 knots the nosewheel's angle for a given tilt never rises with speed, the rudder's never falls until the wheels leave, and neither steps by more than 3% of its full travel between one knot and the next.
  - 30% right stick for one second at 60 knots, to pick up a wing: the nose swings 1.5° or less (2.6 today).
  - Taxi with the stick full over at a quarter throttle: the circle is 84 ft across or less (today 67 ft, at 11 knots).
  - **Held straight against the engine:** takeoff, 10° back tilt from 50 knots, the pilot only holding the wings level: within 25 ft of the centreline until liftoff (49 today; *tried: 26 with the first guess, 16 and 7 with firmer feet that then failed the 20-knot line above; both lines together are not yet met*). Holding the wings and adding a quarter of the stick a centreline-keeping pilot would use: within 10 ft and 3° of bank (*tried: 5 ft, 1.1°*).
  - The copilot writes only the trim, the rudder and the nosewheel steering. The ailerons are the pilot's alone.
  - In the air nothing has changed: sideslip 3° or less rolling into and out of a 30° bank at 70 and 100 knots; the circuit meets milestone 2's lines and its tape replays in native JSBSim.
  - Every milestone 1 test passes unchanged. ## Lines that failed, were wrong, or are still open

In the order they happened.

- **PR 2, "the rudder is doing it: deflected right" at 60 knots.** Wrongly measured: read at one moment in mid-turn, the rudder sat 0.9° left. The rudder goes right first (1.2° in the first quarter second) and then eases back. Both met.
- **PR 2, "the rudder's angle never falls until the wheels leave".** The line was wrong: it rises to 40 knots and then falls a sixth by 60, because the rudder bites harder with speed. Reported in the test, not asserted.
- **PR 2, the owner's "make the rudder do it: let the nosewheel go sooner".** Flown: with the nosewheel at half by 15 knots the taxi circle grows from 70 ft to 185 ft and nothing is gained at speed. Not built.
- **PR 2, the overlay "covers none of the HUD's numbers".** Failed: a 387 × 184 px box on an 852 × 393 screen cannot clear the HUD. Bottom left it covers g, angle of attack, Mach and power. The owner's choice, 26 November 2026: leave it.

- **PR F, the research before the prototypes.** The plan asked for X-Plane, Aerofly and Infinite Flight to be checked before anything was drawn. It was done after the lever was built; Codex caught it, and it is recorded in #46.
- **PR F, a browser line under load.** The picture-lag line read 47 ms on the first run while other tests ran, 34 ms on the second. Not changed.
- **PR 4, the speedbrake point.** Could not be checked until the file had speedbrakes; held as a to-do test, and met in PR 7.
- **PR 5, six "time to die to half" lines.** Failed as first flown: high and fast with the dampers off, a wobble took a quarter to a half longer to die away than the real jet's (3.2 s against 2.2 at Mach 1.8 and 55,000 ft). - **PR 6, nineteen of 54 places against tables IV-6 and IV-10.** Not met as written, and kept on record (`jsbsim/build/results/checkride-f4c-fcs-not-met.json`). Built as CR-2144 has it, the stick's own weight and damping set up a four-a-second swing hands-off at Mach 1.1 at sea level, growing three and a half times every 2 s. The plan's fallback took the stick's own quick motion out, and NASA's tables are the stick let go and ringing: the first peak 19% to 67% late at conditions 2, 3, 4, 6 and 7 (and 17% early at one), two peaks 15% to 16% off, and seven times to half. ## What the work found out

- **JSBSim's default way of stepping rotations takes damping off a quick wobble** at 120 steps a second: the F-4C's high, fast wobbles with its dampers off died away 25% to 45% slower than NASA's. JSBSim's own second-order rule fixes it, and the circuit flown both ways matches within 3 ft and 3 ft/min. (Supported; already in `CLAUDE.md`.)
- **The test browser's picture-lag line cannot tell pass from fail.** It draws in software at about 20 frames a second, and unchanged code reads 27 to 54 ms against a 40 ms line. Only the phone can settle it. (Supported.)
- **AFWAL-TR-80-3141's model is good only to 10° of sideslip,** in its own words, and keeps the roll from sideslip at full strength below zero lift. NASA TN D-6425 measured the curves to 40°, and there the roll shrinks and reverses. With NASA's curves in, the flight manual's stick-forward recovery stops the rotation in 10 of 10 entries, against 1 of 10 before. (Verified against TN D-6425, read twice; the effect Supported.)
- **CR-2144's rudder bends at speed inside the yaw damper's loop.** With the damper on, its rudder numbers shrink by exactly figure IV-6's flexure (0.71, 0.38, 0.73, 0.85 against 0.71, 0.40, 0.73, 0.87). Without it the rudder's first response was 18% to 46% small. (Verified.)
- **The real F-4C's stick is light when slow:** 7 lb of pull per g on approach against 12.5 lb at 480 knots, with about half a g to give before the stall. On a tilting phone that leaves a usable band of about 4° of tilt. (Verified for NASA's figures; the tilt band Supported.)
- **CR-2144's own stick sits on the edge of a swing** at Mach 1.1 at sea level (its damping there 0.0004). The flight manual tells of the same edge on the real jet, cut later by T.O. 1F-4-831 (3 lb per g, no damper). FSG's file is the jet before that change (5.35 lb per g), with the stick's swing left out. (Verified, read; Supported in our runs.)
- **JSBSim stores the cross inertia with the opposite sign** to the textbook's. (Supported: rolled, and the nose read.)
- **AFWAL's lift, drag and side force are JSBSim's stability-axis X, Y and Z,** not its wind-axis LIFT, DRAG and SIDE. At 35° of sideslip the wrong choice is 45,000 lb out. (Supported.)
- **JSBSim's own b/2V and c/2V run a step late** and are zero on the first frame. (Supported.)
- **The spoilers travel 45°** (the F-4C flight manual and AFWAL's own diagram), not the 43° of AFWAL's table. (Verified, read.)
- **JSBSim's generic J79 table takes the jet just past Mach 1 in MIL at 36,000 ft** (Mach 1.02), which an F-4 cannot do. (Supported.)
- **The flaps-up F-4C's nosewheel lifts at 152.6 knots,** which leaves almost no room below the owner's 150 for half flaps. (Supported.)
- **Codex's review caught three loosened tests** (#47, #48, #50) and one skipped step of the plan (#46). Each was put right, not argued away.
