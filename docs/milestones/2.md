## The owner's answers and steer (20 November 2026)

He first chose his own `C172.glb`, converted by script, on `c172x` with its elevator's slack removed. Shown the first draft, he steered: **look at FlightGear's Cessna model; if it is more accurate and, most of all, pairs more exactly with JSBSim's Cessna and works well, borrow that, and don't bluff with a rough model on the wrong geometry.** And: **stop at a good stopping point, the end of PR 1.**

**What was found** (Supported: all three models and both aircraft files measured, JSBSim's numbers read back from runs):

| Wheels, from JSBSim's centre of gravity as loaded | Wheelbase | Main wheels out from the centreline |
|---|---|---|
| JSBSim's `c172x` | 65.0 in | 50.25 in |
| JSBSim's `c172p` | 65.0 in | 43.0 in |
| FlightGear's model ([c172p-team](https://github.com/c172p-team/c172p), `Models/c172-common.ac`) | 64.8 in | 43.7 in |
| The owner's model | 69.3 in | 50.0 in |

- **FlightGear's model fits JSBSim's `c172p` within 0.7 in everywhere.** They are the same airplane: JSBSim's bundled `c172p` came from FlightGear's.
- It fits `c172x` no better than the owner's does: right wheelbase, wheels 6.5 in too far in. His has the right track and a wheelbase 4.3 in long. (I told him at first that his nose wheel was the one out. That was measured from the empty airplane's balance point in the file. JSBSim flies it loaded.)
- **`c172p` is also the better airplane, as shipped:**
  - no slack in its elevator: hands off it holds 100.0 to 100.1 knots, and started 20 knots off it is back within 5 in about a minute;
  - a nosewheel the stick can steer, so the game needs no trick with the wheel brakes;
  - full back stick gives 4.1 g at 100 knots, against `c172x`'s 5.2;
  - it flew milestone 1's whole circuit on the first try, with the Cessna pilot's numbers unchanged: liftoff at 60 knots 0.9 ft off the centreline, downwind 927 to 993 ft at 90 to 91 knots, touchdown at 183 ft/min 1,391 ft down the runway and 1.7 ft off the centreline, stopped at 1,934 ft.
- **What FlightGear's model is:** an AC3D file of 48,968 triangles with its interior, every moving part a named piece, three 2,048-pixel paint textures. Its motions are written in FlightGear's own format beside it, not in the model.
- **What borrowing it costs:**
  - **Its licence is the GPL, version 2.** The converted model stays GPL: the app credits it, and the model's source and our conversion script must be there for anyone who has the app. They are, in this repo. The usual reading is that the rest of the app is not touched by this, as with JSBSim's own LGPL data already here. I am not a lawyer. If the repo is private, say so before PR 2.
  - **A bigger conversion than his own model needed:** read AC3D, leave out the interior, bring the textures down to 1,024 pixels, and write the handoff's motions from FlightGear's hinge lines. Still a script in the repo, no Blender.

**So the plan is: FlightGear's model on JSBSim's `c172p`, flown as JSBSim ships it.** Nothing is patched: his "slack removed" answer is met by the file itself. His own model stays in Drive, untouched. This is an exception to "models are never built by Claude Code", for this stand-in: a copy is converted by script.

**A button shows only if the airplane has the thing** (his third answer, unchanged). The Cessna shows Flaps and Brakes. The F-4C gets all four in milestone 3.

**This session stops when PR 1 (this plan) is up and reviewed.** The rest is picked up when he says.

## 1. How others solved this

### The picture between physics steps
| What others do | Label |
|---|---|
| Glenn Fiedler: draw between the last two physics states, never the newest alone; for states that arrive from elsewhere, draw a fixed time behind and blend the two states either side of that moment. Blend attitude on the sphere (slerp), and do not guess ahead: "extrapolation doesn't work very well for rigid bodies". [Fix Your Timestep](https://gafferongames.com/post/fix_your_timestep/), [Snapshot Interpolation](https://gafferongames.com/post/snapshot_interpolation/) | Verified (read) |
| WebFlight (JSBSim in a Worker at 120 Hz, our setup): "drawing the latest snapshot each frame moved the glider by 0, 1 or 2 steps from one frame to the next … and the chase camera, fixed to it, shook the whole world." Its fix: a picture clock that trails the newest state by 25 ms and is eased toward it, blending between the two states either side. Instruments use the newest state. [pose-smoother.ts](https://github.com/rtnicolae/WebFlight/blob/HEAD/apps/web/src/pose-smoother.ts) | Verified (read) |
| FlightDynamicsLab and opengeo-sim draw the newest state with no blending. [FlightDynamicsLab](https://github.com/tkir97/FlightDynamicsLab), [opengeo-sim](https://github.com/jorisfalter/opengeo-sim) | Verified (read) |
| Wings and Rage: the simulation ran on the page, and the picture was blended between its last two steps. | Verified (the owner's phone, in Wings) |
| Planning runs, with states arriving about as the owner's phone delivers them: drawing the newest state, every frame at 60 a second is a whole step (8.3 ms) out. A clock two steps (16.7 ms) behind and eased gently is within 0.7 ms a frame at 60 and 1.1 ms at 30 (Low Power Mode), and about 25 ms behind real time. | Supported (two runs: mine and the review's) |
| The same run with WebFlight's easing exactly as written: its clock settles one frame ahead of where its 25 ms says, so at 30 frames a second it sits on the newest state and is a step out on most frames. | Supported (the formula as quoted, in our run; WebFlight itself was not run) |
| Posting about 350 bytes a state costs nothing that matters: payloads up to 10 KB fit a frame's budget even on slow phones. [Surma](https://surma.dev/things/is-postmessage-slow/) | Verified (read); the iPhone figure is Reasoned, settled by PR 5's phone check |

**Choice.** WebFlight's method: the page keeps the last states, and a picture clock trails the newest one. Position is blended in a straight line and attitude on the sphere (Fiedler, and Wings). Everything else (HUD, surfaces, propeller) reads the newest state. Two departures, each with its reason: the easing is worked out after the clock has moved on, not before, so it holds in Low Power Mode; and the clock trails by two steps, not 25 ms, because that is enough on the owner's phone and the hands feel every millisecond.

The clock keeps four rules, each with a test: it never shows a moment ahead of the newest state; it never goes backwards; more than 50 ms behind, it jumps forward; while the game is held, it stands still.

### Tilt as a stick, on an iPhone
| What others do | Label |
|---|---|
| The phone's three orientation angles go unstable ("gimbal lock") when the phone is held upright. The W3C spec says so and gives the sums for a proper rotation. [W3C](https://www.w3.org/TR/orientation-event/) | Verified (read) |
| Wings' `tilt.js`: works out which way is "up" on the screen from the angles and the screen's rotation, so both landscapes work by one rule and the compass heading drops out. Neutral is wherever the phone is at Fly or Recenter. Full stick at 25° of pitch and 30° of roll, a dead zone of 2° and 1.5°, a straight line between, and a 20 ms smoothing. | Verified (the owner's phone, in Wings) |
| Infinite Flight's "Calibrate" does the same: neutral is however you hold the phone, from the pause menu. [forum](https://community.infiniteflight.com/t/what-does-calibrate-mean/901400) | From a forum post; not the maker's own page |
| iOS asks for motion permission only inside a tap, and WebKit "only remembers for the lifetime of the web page": a home-screen app asks at every launch. [w3c/deviceorientation#74](https://github.com/w3c/deviceorientation/issues/74), [WebKit 213468](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=213468) | Verified (read) |
| The screen's rotation angle changed meaning across iOS 16.x. Read it at every change and test both landscapes on the phone. [WebKit 254863](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=254863), [255388](https://bugs.webkit.org/show_bug.cgi?id=255388) | Verified (read) |
| How many degrees X-Plane Mobile, Aerofly or PocketWings take for full stick: nothing published. | Not found |
| Found in the review: one reading that is not a number leaves Wings' stick stuck at not-a-number, and milestone 1 found that such a control ends the flight. | Supported (the review's run) |

**Choice.** Wings' `tilt.js` with its numbers, copied with one change: a reading that is not a number is ignored. He has flown it. How it feels on a JSBSim airplane is his to say after the first flight. The controls go to the Worker as each sensor reading and each touch arrives, not once a frame, so Low Power Mode adds no delay to the hands.

### The chase camera
| What others do | Label |
|---|---|

### The Cessna's flaws

**Rules the design keeps**
- **Milestone 1 is not touched.** `C172X` stays a test aircraft, flown by milestone 1's tests against native, and the list of 34 state numbers stays as it is. The game flies `c172p`. JSBSim's data is not edited, and nothing is patched as it loads.
- **A flight is still a start and a tape.** The page sends only the seven controls.
- **The simulation code stays free of DOM and Three.js.** Page-side sums (the picture's clock, the axes, tilt, HUD text) are plain modules too, tested in Node. What needs a browser is checked in headless Chromium.
- **The model's surfaces show what JSBSim's surfaces do,** from the state: each surface as a fraction of its own full travel in JSBSim, shown as that fraction of the model's travel. (JSBSim counts its rudder the other way from the handoff. The aircraft's settings carry each surface's travel and sign, and a "done" line checks each surface on screen.)
- **Altitude reads at the wheels,** as in Wings: 0 on the runway.
- **A restart is a new flight** (48 to 53 ms in Node). The picture's clock starts again from its first state; states from the flight before are dropped.
- **Any ending shows the crashed screen** with Restart: a hard arrival or water with the override off, a broken state always, and a Worker that has failed.
- **Copied files say where they came from** (repo, path, commit, per file), and `provenance.json` lists them.
